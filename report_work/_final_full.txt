0044|Heading 1|Abstract
0045|Normal|
0046|Normal|Despite the modernization of the healthcare sector, the appointment scheduling for local clinics remains largely manual, with most of them using phones to register patients and having to deal with walk-in queues, which not only creates inefficiency but also makes the access to clinics difficult for patients. This project is a comprehensive Digital Appointment Management System built with a full-stack application, using Next.js for the Web Dashboard, React Native for the mobile App, NestJS RESTful backend, and PostgreSQL relational database, supporting both clinical staff and administrators.
0047|Normal|
0048|Normal|The research question for this project is: How effective is a digital booking system in enhancing the availability of medical checkup packages for local clinics and patients? The implementation of the system, literature review, and system design and empirical evaluation using a structured user survey shows that the digital booking system can make it much more convenient for patients, shorten their perceived waiting time, and make clinic management more efficient.
0049|Normal|
0050|Normal|The system is designed with a three-tier architecture, integrates JWT based authentication, RESTful APIs and soft-delete data management to satisfy both functional requirements and legal obligation of data protection under GDPR. Overall, user feedback through the survey scored high satisfaction in the areas of convenience, accessibility, security and digital booking experience. The project finds that digital scheduling systems are a significant improvement over current scheduling techniques and could be improved further by using predictive scheduling algorithms and extending their use to larger contexts.

0054|Heading 2|Background of the Project
0055|Normal|Clinical operations are one of the primary tasks that are performed in a healthcare organization. In most developed and developing countries manual scheduling (walking and talking) is still a primary method of scheduling in small and local clinics. The following methods are clearly inefficient, causing conflicts in schedules, long waiting times of patients, administrative costs and eventually, impacting on the quality of the care provided to patients (Humaid and Ghazali, 2022).
0056|Normal|The increasing digitalization of the healthcare industry has presented the chance to completely reimagine the patient booking process. In academic studies, mobile apps have been demonstrated to cut down waiting times, enhance patient satisfaction and improve administrative efficiency in clinical environments (Nanteza, 2025; Azhari and Nor, 2023). In small community-based clinics, however, these systems have not yet been developed and there is a definite need to be filled by this project.

0092|Heading 1|Chapter 2: Literature Review
0093|Heading 2|2.1 Introduction to Digital Healthcare Appointment Systems
0094|Normal|Over the last ten years, healthcare administration has increasingly been studied digitally, with a special interest in the appointment scheduling process as a critical operational area. The inefficiency and poor patient satisfaction with traditional systems using telephone registration, in-person walk-in registration and paper records etc. are widely documented (Humaid and Ghazali, 2022). This digitalization, via web-based booking portals, mobile apps and automated notification systems, has garnered significant academic interest as a way to boost patient satisfaction and streamline the processes of healthcare providers.
0095|Normal|This literature review critically analyzes some of the main research in this field, exploring what has been found concerning the positive, as well as the negative, aspects of digital appointment systems, the role of mobile or web technologies, the importance of security and authentication, and the new role of algorithmic scheduling. The review will help to identify the gap in the research that this project will be addressing.
0096|Normal|
0097|Heading 2|2.2 Limitations of Traditional Appointment Systems
0098|Normal|It is well known in the literature that the fundamental problem that is tackled in this project is present. Humaid and Ghazali (2022) give a comprehensive study of the challenges of the manual booking mechanism in the medical clinic environment. Their research shows that time and costs are important for patients when they book a test using physical and telephone channels especially in geographically remote and time limited situations. They saw a significant improvement in patient convenience and clinic volume by using their online booking system.
0099|Normal|Azhari and Nor(2023), continue this research and work on authentication and secure management of calendar in digital clinic appointment system. The have excellent effects for the dual needs of efficiency and security in health care settings. They report that systems with powerful authentication methods achieve greater patient trust and improve the reliability of patient management results. The results are directly relevant to the design decisions that were taken in this project, particularly for the decision to use JWT-based authentication.
0100|Normal|The study carried out at Makerere University by Nanteza (2025) expands the geographies under consideration in this evidence base to show that manual appointment scheduling is not a problem only in high income context of healthcare. The double booking, communication and scheduling inefficiencies were found to be common issues in the studied institution. However, it was decided that digital booking systems could be used to solve these problems to a large degree, giving weight to the question of whether or not this project would be necessary in a local clinic setting.
0101|Normal|
0102|Heading 2|
0103|Heading 2|2.3 Mobile Healthcare Applications
0104|Normal|One of the most recent areas of study focuses on what role mobile apps play in improving a patient's access to healthcare. The studies on Android-based healthcare apps highlight how they improve patient engagement by allowing for the on-demand scheduling of appointments, sending notifications and communication with healthcare providers (1SAFINAAZ, 2026). The fact that booking functions are provided 24/7 is designated as of special importance where the working hours of clinics restrict the availability of traditional booking functions.
0105|Normal|Jalleh (2022) has carried out a comprehensive study on mobile hospital appointment system with QR-based check-in, real-time queue tracking and automated appointment reminders and the research work is from University Tunku Abdul Rahman (UTAR). The results demonstrate that if these functionalities are performed well, they can lead to tangible reductions in patient waiting times and increased satisfaction with the appointment experience from the patient’s perspective. This study will be valuable as a design precedent for the mobile application aspect of the current project, which includes the use of appointment notifications and live status updates.
0106|Heading 2|2.4 Web-Based Systems and Administrative Efficiency
0107|Normal|Hospital (2026) determined that web based hospital registration and scheduling system has been shown to increase the effectiveness of administration because of the elimination of the scheduling issue, real time access to data and better resource tracking. Online platforms make administrative workloads and manual record keeping easy and give clinical managers an up-to-the-minute overview of capacity and patient flow.
0108|Normal|IEEE Kosalairaman et al. (2025) demonstrate the advantages of smart healthcare appointment systems in the institutional level with real-time data and communication technologies. The results of the project support the idea of having a tight coupling between the patient-facing and the administrator-facing parts of the system, both sharing the same data layer, as in the current project’s NestJS and PostgreSQL backend.
0109|Heading 2|2.5 Communication Technologies in Healthcare Scheduling 
0110|Normal|One of the interesting aspects of the literature is the development of communication channels for the purposes of appointment management. Alanazy (n.d.) explores how WhatsApp can be used as a communication and bookings platform for patients and health care providers. The results show increased efficiency in communicating and coordinating tasks; however, there are limitations in data structure, searchability and integration with formal health care records systems. The project in question takes a purpose-built approach to achieve the communication aspects of the platforms using automated push notifications, while retaining the data integrity and security of a formal application.
0111|Heading 2|2.6 Patient Satisfaction and Adoption Barriers
0112|Normal|This positive effect of digital appointments on patient satisfaction has been a constant theme throughout the literature. Warif (2023) after the implementation of the system reported large shifts towards digital booking in hospital contexts with reductions in waiting times and improvements in service quality ratings. In addition to that, the study exposes challenges to adoption such as lack of technical expertise, volatility in internet connectivity and awareness of users. The results are of particular relevance to the design of the existing system, especially with regards to the mobile interface (which needs to be clean and user friendly) and the booking process itself (which needs to be simple from a user's point of view).
0113|Normal|Ming-Sheng Lee et al. (2020) draws on the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) and the information system quality models to conclude that high adoption rates in digital health systems are achieved primarily by user-friendliness, real-time scheduling and security of data management. The current project is directly influenced by this piece of evidence.
0114|Normal|
0115|Heading 2|2.7 Algorithmic and Predictive Scheduling 
0116|Normal|The literature in the field of algorithmic or machine learning approaches to healthcare scheduling is still growing, albeit not as wide-ranging as in other fields. In this context, the literature on the use of machine learning for medical appointment scheduling is very useful and critically considers possible algorithmic bias, such as that of Samorani et al. (2021). The IEEE article by Kosalairaman et al. (2025) also discusses how data-driven scheduling can be used to make dynamic scheduling decisions from historical data on consultation time.
0117|Normal|The present project is of the goal to find the best way to schedule doctor consultations which means that the project’s design dream is to learn from the past booking times of doctors and to best adapt the intervals for booking consultations. This feature is not quite in place yet in this project, but its logical extension would be to further develop this project and is incorporated into this new literature.
0118|Heading 2|2.8 Research Gap 
0119|Normal|Key findings from the literature reviewed include: digital booking of appointments has positive effects on patient access and convenience; mobile apps are potentially more effective in terms of patient engagement; there is a disconnect between technology that is available and seems to be adopted locally/community level in clinics; and patient identification and access to real-time data are essential.
0120|Normal|The majority of current systems (including those reviewed in the literature) are geared toward a simple Web-based booking application or a large hospital setting. The available research that is specific to integrated multi-platform systems for small local clinics that have an integrated patient mobile application, clinician web dashboard, and secure API layer in one environment is still limited. This is the main reason for the present project.

0123|Heading 2|2.9 Summary
0124|Normal|The current project is a continuation of this literature review, which has created the evidence base for the project. There are well known shortcomings of conventional appointment systems. There is clear evidence that digital platforms, whether on the web or mobile, enhance accessibility for patients and clinic productivity. Security, usability and real-time functionality are critical design requirements. This project's main contribution is the development of an integrated, local-clinic focused digital scheduling system, which is supported by the research gap identified.
